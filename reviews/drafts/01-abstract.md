44 \begin{abstract}
45 Water demand in the United States is characterized by significant sectoral and regional heterogeneity, driven by the coupled evolution of climate, land use, population, and economic activity. We present a gridded (1/8$^{\circ}$), monthly, multi-sector water demand dataset for the contiguous United States (CONUS) covering 1980--2100 across eight scenarios. Our analysis reveals that while aggregate CONUS-scale annual demand aligns within 10\% of USGS 2015 estimates, this close agreement reflects the partial cancellation of significant sectoral biases: a -45\% underestimate in domestic demand and a -30\% underestimate in thermoelectric withdrawals are largely offset by a +5\% bias in irrigation. This dataset improves upon previous global products by incorporating state-resolved sectoral demands from the Global Change Analysis Model (GCAM-USA), explicit future power plant siting via the CERF model, and scenario-consistent high-resolution climate and population forcing. By capturing the fine-scale spatiotemporal patterns of demand across diverse futures, this dataset supports integrated energy-water-land modeling and local-to-regional water scarcity assessments.
46 \end{abstract}
